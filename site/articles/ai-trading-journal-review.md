AI is good at one part of trading improvement: reading a pile of results and telling you, plainly, what stands out. It's bad at another: doing arithmetic on a messy spreadsheet without making things up. A good AI review plays to the first strength and avoids the second.

## The rule: give it numbers, not homework

If you paste 200 rows and ask "how did I do?", the AI has to calculate everything itself, and it may round, skip or invent. Instead, give it the **statistics already calculated**, plus the trades for context:

- Net P&L, win rate, average R, profit factor, largest win and loss, losing streak
- Results by setup, weekday, hour, mistake and emotion, with trade counts
- Plan followed vs rule broken
- The trade list for the period

Then tell it to **use only these numbers**.

## A prompt that works

> You are my trading coach. Below is my trading journal for the period. Use only these numbers: don't invent figures and don't give buy/sell signals or predictions.
>
> Write my review in this order:
> 1. The period in 3 lines (net P&L, win rate, average R, profit factor).
> 2. What worked: the setups, hours or days that made money, with numbers.
> 3. What cost me: the mistakes and emotions that lost money, with numbers.
> 4. Discipline: trades that followed my plan vs trades that broke it.
> 5. One concrete rule for next week, based on the data.
>
> Keep it short and direct. If a group has fewer than 5 trades, say the sample is small.

## What a good review looks like

From a sample journal, last 14 days:

> **The period:** 12 trades, net +$385.28, win rate 67%, average +0.56R, profit factor 3.80.
> **What worked:** 10:00–12:00, 8 trades, 7 wins, +0.91R average.
> **What cost you:** one FOMO entry, −$2.48. Small amount, but it's the habit that matters.
> **Discipline:** 11 trades followed the plan (+$387.76); 1 broke it.
> **One rule:** no trades before 10:00 next week.

Short, specific, built on real numbers, and it ends with something you can actually do.

## Ten good questions to ask

1. Rank my setups by average R. Flag any with fewer than 20 trades.
2. What did breaking my plan cost me this month?
3. Which hour of the day costs me the most?
4. What happens to my next trade after a loss?
5. Am I cutting winners short? Compare average win and average loss in R.
6. Which emotion shows up before my worst trades?
7. Compare this month with last month.
8. Do I overtrade on red days?
9. Which mistake should I fix first, by money lost?
10. What's my longest losing streak, and how deep was the drawdown?
