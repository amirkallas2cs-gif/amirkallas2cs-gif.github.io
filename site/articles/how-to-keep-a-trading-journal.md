Most traders start a journal and stop within two weeks. The usual reason: it asks for too much. A journal you can't keep up is worse than none, because it gives you a false picture. Here's a version that takes about thirty seconds per trade.

## The 10 fields to log

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Date and time | Shows your best and worst hours and weekdays |
| Instrument | Point values differ: MES, ES, NQ, stocks, forex, crypto |
| Setup | The only way to know which of your ideas actually work |
| Side | Long and short often behave very differently |
| Entry | Needed for P&L and R |
| Stop | Defines your risk, and therefore your R |
| Exit | Needed for P&L and R |
| Size | Contracts, shares or lots |
| Fees | Part of the real result |
| Followed my plan? | Yes or no: the most honest field in the journal |

Everything else, such as risk, R multiple, P&L, result, equity and drawdown, should be **calculated**, not typed. Typing derived numbers is where journals go wrong.

## Three optional fields that pay off

**Mistake.** Pick from a short list: FOMO entry, moved my stop, oversized, exited too early, revenge trade, chased price, no setup. After a month you'll know which one costs you the most.

**Emotion.** Calm, confident, anxious, impatient, frustrated, bored, excited. It feels soft, but the numbers are often brutal. In one sample journal, 55 calm trades made +$1,994 while 6 frustrated trades lost −$674.

**A one-line note.** Context you'll want in three months: news, a missed rule, a lesson.

## What to look at every week

1. **Net P&L, win rate, average R, profit factor.** Are you better or worse than your usual?
2. **Plan discipline.** Trades that followed your plan vs trades that broke it. If the second group loses money, and it usually does, you have a clear rule to work on.
3. **Setups.** Average R by setup, with the number of trades. Cut or fix what loses.
4. **Time.** Results by hour and weekday. Many traders find one window where most of the damage happens.
5. **One rule for next week.** Just one. "No trades before 10:00." "Max two trades after a loss."

## Weekly review with AI

You can hand your journal to an AI and ask for the review. It works well if the AI gets **real numbers** instead of guessing. Give it the statistics (or let a tool calculate them first) and ask for a short, structured review: the period in three lines, what worked, what cost you, discipline, and one rule. ## Spreadsheet or app?

A spreadsheet works if you're disciplined and comfortable with formulas. The usual problems: broken formulas, copy-paste errors, and a dashboard you have to rebuild every time you want a new view.

## Make it stick

- **Log right after closing the trade**, not at the end of the day.
- **Keep the required fields to ten.** Optional fields are optional.
- **Review weekly, at the same time**, for twenty minutes.
- **Never edit history to look better.** The journal is for you.
