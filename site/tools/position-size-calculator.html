Size is rounded down to whole units. For forex, the result is in standard lots (0.01 = micro lot).

How the calculator works

Position sizing answers one question: if your stop is hit, how much do you lose? You decide that amount first, as a percentage of your account. Then the size follows from the distance to your stop and from what one point is worth.

Size = (balance × risk %) ÷ (|entry − stop| × point value)

Example: a $10,000 account risking 1% has $100 at risk. A long MES at 5,000 with a stop at 4,996 is a 4-point stop. One MES point is worth $5, so each contract risks $20, and you can trade 5 contracts.

Keeping the same risk on every trade is what makes your results comparable. It's also what makes the R multiple meaningful: a +2R trade means the same thing whether you traded 1 contract or 10.
